Small model, honest numbers

How far can a 22M-parameter encoder get on textual entailment, what does a big teacher actually transfer, and where do the parameters really go?

1 · THE TASK

Does the premise make the hypothesis true?

premiseDana Reeve, the widow of the actor Christopher Reeve, has died of lung cancer at age 44, according to the Christopher Reeve Foundation.
hypothesisChristopher Reeve had an accident.
gold labelnot_entailment  true in the world, but the premise never says it

RTE is small (2.5k training pairs) and hard: word overlap alone gets 57.4% on dev and the majority class gets 52.7%. The challenge grades accuracy together with parameter count, so the question is never just “what scores highest”.

2 · THE PLAN

One pipeline, one code path, only the config changes

Amber = teacher side, indigo = student side. Dev (277) is touched only to report numbers; every choice is made on a 10% validation split of train, grouped by premise so near-duplicate pairs never straddle it.

  • Every run: 5 seeds, early stopping on validation, layer-wise LR decay 0.9, separate head LR, max length 256.
  • Each run writes its config, package versions, parameter counts, per-seed metrics and dev predictions.
  • Teacher and the final student trained on Colab (T4); a Colab rerun of the submitted seed is bit-identical, an Apple-MPS rerun is not (dev 74.7 vs 78.0).
3 · TEACHER SOFT LABELS

Never let the teacher grade its own homework

A model's logits on examples it trained on are near-certain and carry no “dark knowledge”. So the soft labels come out of fold: five teachers, each predicting only the fifth of train it never saw.

Reproducibility check: two independent Colab runs produced byte-identical teacher logits.

4 · DISTILLATION

Gold labels plus the teacher's opinion

plain student77.7dev, mean of 5 seeds (± 2.1)
distilled, α 0.5 · T 276.8± 1.6 · paired t, p = 0.27 (not significant)
what KD did improve0.74dev log-loss, down from 0.99; seed agreement 92% vs 88%

Plain distillation made the student better calibrated and more stable, not more accurate.

Why? The teacher has a bias the student copies. It predicts entailment on 57.8% of train against 50.2% gold, and 72% of its errors are on not-entailment pairs. The distilled student says entailment 61.5% of the time.

5 · WHERE THE PARAMETERS ARE

70% of the small model is a lookup table

Two ways to shrink it, two very different outcomes:

rank-128 factorised embedding51.038.1M params · dev ± 5.0 · near chance

SVD at rank 128 keeps only 57.5% of the embedding's energy. Training loss sat at 0.70 (chance) for 4–5 epochs on every seed: the network had to relearn language from 2.5k examples.

vocabulary pruning78.345.5M params · submitted checkpoint (full: 78.0)

Keep only the rows that matter, unchanged. Applied after training, no retraining. Outputs are bit-identical on any input made of kept tokens.

6 · VOCABULARY PRUNING

Keep the rows you will use, send the rest to [UNK]

How many frequent tokens to keep is decided on train only: half of train must have 98% of its tokens covered by the other half plus the top-N list. That rule picks N = 60,000. Dev is only used to draw the curve below.

Submitted checkpoint (masked KD, seed 21). On the plain seed-21 checkpoint the same cut cost 0.7 points; here it gained 0.4, i.e. it is within noise either way. The tail of unseen tokens is mostly names (Hutchinson, Bakr, Nottinghamshire), so cutting harder costs real accuracy.

−36% parameters at no measurable accuracy cost, decided without looking at dev.

7 · RESULTS

Accuracy against size

Doubling the student (xsmall → small) bought nothing. The 7-point gap to the teacher is about pretraining depth and width, not raw parameter count; most of small's extra parameters are, again, embedding rows.

9 · TAKEAWAYS

What I would tell the next person

  • Count where the parameters live. In small encoders the vocabulary dominates; pruning it is nearly free, factorising it is not.
  • A biased teacher passes on its bias. Plain KD improved calibration and stability but not accuracy; masking the teacher's wrong labels removed the bias and gave the best student, though within seed noise.
  • Bigger is not automatically better on 2.5k examples. v3-small (2× params) tied xsmall.
  • Report means over seeds and test significance. A ±2-point seed spread is larger than most of the effects we chased.
  • Pin the hardware too. The same seed and code reproduced bit for bit on Colab and drifted 3 points on Apple MPS.
  • Next with more compute: intermediate-layer distillation, balancing the teacher's labels before distilling, and NLI-style data augmentation from the training pairs themselves.
Code, configs and every run's metrics are in the submitted code archive. Models: microsoft/deberta-v3-base, -small, -xsmall (pretrained only, no RTE/NLI fine-tuned checkpoints). Data: GLUE RTE.
