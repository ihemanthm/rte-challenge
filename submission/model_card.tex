{\Large\bfseries Model card: pruned DeBERTa-v3-xsmall for RTE}\\[2pt]
{\small [Student name] $\cdot$ [Roll number] $\cdot$ CS6315E RTE Challenge \\ code: \url{https://github.com/ihemanthm/rte-challenge}}

\textbf{Architecture.} \texttt{microsoft/deberta-v3-xsmall} encoder (12 layers, hidden 384, 6 heads) + pooler + linear 2-way head. Full fine-tuning; \textbf{no} LoRA/adapters, \textbf{no} distillation in the final model. After training, the input word-embedding is \emph{vocabulary-pruned}: only the rows for the 62,245 kept tokens (special tokens $\cup$ every training-set token $\cup$ the 60,000 most frequent tokens of the tokenizer's unigram model) are kept; any other token id maps to \texttt{[UNK]} through a fixed lookup table.

\textbf{Training data.} RTE train (2,490 pairs; identical to GLUE RTE train), minus a 10\% validation split grouped by premise. No external NLI/RTE data, no pre-fine-tuned RTE checkpoints. Dev (GLUE RTE validation, 277) is used only for reporting.

\textbf{Intended use and limits.} Binary entailment on short English news-style pairs. Text with tokens outside the kept vocabulary loses those tokens to \texttt{[UNK]} (98.5\% of dev tokens are kept; on the 60\% of dev pairs that are fully covered, outputs equal the unpruned model exactly). Known weaknesses: over-predicts entailment (57.4\% vs.\ 52.7\% gold), high-overlap non-entailments, modality/quantifiers, number binding.
